# Interview notes for Agent Wei

This is the source material for the "Talk to Wei" agent. Everything you write here may be
repeated to a visitor, so only include what you are happy for a recruiter or hiring manager to hear.
The repo is public on GitHub, so treat this file as public too.

How to fill it in
- Answer in your own words, the way you would say it out loud. Bullet points are fine.
- Aim for 3 to 6 sentences per answer. Specifics beat polish: names of decisions, numbers, what went wrong.
- Skip anything you do not want to answer. Delete the question or leave it blank.
- Best option: record yourself answering Parts B, C and D out loud (see "Recording plan" at the bottom).
  The same recording trains your voice clone and gives us a transcript in your natural speaking style.

---

## Part A. Quick facts the bot needs

- What kind of role are you open to next? (IC, lead, founding designer, fractional, not looking)
- Company stage and type you want (early startup, growth stage, big tech, agency)
- Location and remote preference
- Earliest start date, or "not actively looking"
- Things you are NOT interested in
- Work authorization status, if you want the bot to answer this. Otherwise leave blank and it will decline.

## Part B. Your story

1. "Tell me about yourself" in 30 seconds, the version you actually use.
2. Why product design? What got you into it?
3. Why growth design in particular? What do you like about it?
4. Why did you leave Docusign, and why Illoca and Lofi now?
5. What is the split between Illoca and Lofi in a normal week?
6. Where do you want to be in 3 to 5 years?
7. What is your superpower as a designer? What would a past manager say it is?
8. What is a real weakness, and what are you doing about it?
9. What is the work you are proudest of, and why that one?

## Part C. Project deep dives

Answer the same five prompts for each project below. These are the questions interviewers actually ask.

Prompts
- a. What was the real problem, and how did you frame it? What was the business asking for versus what you decided to solve?
- b. What was the hardest decision or tradeoff? What options did you consider and why did you pick the one you did?
- c. Where did you get pushback (PM, eng, leadership, legal), and how did you handle it?
- d. What went wrong or what would you do differently?
- e. How did you know it worked? How did you measure it, and what surprised you in the data?

## Part D. Behavioral stories

Give one concrete story each. Situation, what you did, the result. Real names are not needed.

1. A time you disagreed with a PM or engineer and how it resolved.
2. A time you were handed something vague or ambiguous and how you made it concrete.
3. A project that failed or got killed. What happened and what did you take from it?
4. Hard feedback you received and what you changed because of it.
5. A time you influenced a decision without having authority over it.
6. A time you had to ship under a tight deadline and what you cut.
7. A time you mentored or grew someone (UX East Meets West counts).
8. A time you had to say no to a stakeholder.

## Part E. Craft and process

1. How do you start a new project? What do you do in the first week?
2. How do you decide what to research versus what to just ship and learn from?
3. How do you pick metrics for a design? Give an example.
4. How do you work with data scientists and analysts?
5. How do you think about design systems and consistency versus speed?
6. What is your quality bar? When is something "done"?
7. How exactly do you use AI in your day-to-day design work? Which tools, for what, and where it fails you.
8. What is your view on designers who code? Where is the line for you?
9. How do you run a workshop or a usability session? Anything you do differently from the textbook?

## Part F. Working with you

1. How do you like to receive feedback?
2. What does your ideal team look like? Size, roles, rituals.
3. What do you need from a manager?
4. What kind of work drains you?
5. Remote, hybrid, or in office, and why?
6. How do you handle disagreement with leadership?
7. What do people misread about you at first?

## Part G. Off limits and escalation

List anything the bot must not answer. Suggested defaults, edit as you like:
- Salary expectations and past compensation: decline, say "that is a conversation for Wei directly", give email.
- References: decline, offer email.
- Confidential details about Illoca, Docusign, or Meta beyond what is on the site.
- Anything about visa or work authorization (unless answered in Part A).
- Health, family, politics, religion.

What should the bot say when it does not know? Write the exact sentence you want it to use.

## Part H. Voice and personality

1. How do you talk? Casual or formal, short or long, do you swear, do you joke?
2. Phrases you actually use a lot.
3. Things you would never say, or words you dislike.
4. How should the bot open the conversation? Write the first line.
5. Write two sample answers in your own voice so the bot can copy the style:
   - "What are you working on right now?"
   - "What is the pricing page project about?"
6. Should the bot ever ask the visitor questions back (who they are, what role, what company)?
7. Disclosure line the bot says if asked whether it is really Wei. Suggested:
   "I am an AI version of Wei, trained on his notes and voice. For anything important, email him at weihsunc@gmail.com."

## Recording plan (voice clone plus content in one session)

- Quiet room, no music or fan, phone close to your mouth or a USB mic. Record in one or two takes.
- Talk like you are in a real interview: natural pace, pauses are fine, no reading in a flat voice.
- Answer Parts B, C and D out loud. That is easily 30 to 45 minutes, which is what a professional clone needs.
- Save as WAV or high bitrate M4A and drop the file (or a link) in the project. I will transcribe it,
  turn the transcript into the knowledge file, and prepare the clips for ElevenLabs.

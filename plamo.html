Goal

Illoca started with Tracing Paper, an AI drafting tool for architects. When Plamo pulled stronger traction with EPC and BIM teams, the company bet on it. My job: make it a product people can discover, try, understand and pay for, with geometry they can trust and keep editing.

My role

All design for Plamo, from the first marketing touchpoint to the core modeling experience.

  • Pivot: Reframed the product from a 2D architect canvas to an agentic 3D workspace, under one brand with two products.
  • Brand and marketing: Directed an external agency, owned messaging and positioning, revamped illoca.com to front Plamo.
  • Product: Sign up, onboarding, first model, the workspace and the upgrade flow.
  • Monetization: The credit system and plan tiers (Free, Explore, Team, Enterprise).

The pivot

The demos that turned into real conversations were the ones where a sketch became a model someone could open in Rhino the same day. Customer learning showed Plamo pulling strongly from the GC, EPC, developer and owner side, so we kept the shared foundations, gave each product its own audience, and made Plamo the lead.

Brand and positioning

Plamo lives under the illoca brand: blueprint blue, rust orange and warm paper grounds that read like a drafting table. I directed an external agency on the identity and the marketing site, owned the positioning against Revit, Motif and Navisworks, and revamped illoca.com so Plamo is what visitors meet first.

Problems

  • Trust: AI 3D usually means a dead mesh. These users need geometry they can keep editing.
  • Learning curve: They know Revit and Rhino, not prompting.
  • Agent vs. controls: What belongs to the agent, what to the panels, and does the agent know what you changed by hand?
  • Value before paywall: Credits are abstract. Users need to feel what one buys before they upgrade.

Design directions

Five moments carry the experience. Each one got its own design pass.

01

Sign up to first model

1,000 starter credits and a guided first prompt get users to editable geometry in a median of 9 minutes.

02

Agentic planning

The agent shows its plan before building, so users can steer before credits are spent.

03

Parametric editing

Tweaks, layers, section cuts and saved views keep the model a working file, not a render.

04

Seamless export

Rhino, Grasshopper, SketchUp, STEP, DWG and more, with layer selection, so Plamo fits the tools teams already use.

05

Upgrade

Prompts appear at natural limits and lead with what you unlock. The credit meter separates monthly credits from purchased ones. Every upgrader visited pricing in the 24 hours before paying, most of them mid-session.

Two bars, so users see when paid credits start. The limit shows up where the user is working.

  • 2 daysmedian from sign up to paid upgrade, self-serve MRR up 17% month over month
  • 263companies, mostly AEC firms
  • 1,121models exported, Rhino first at 43%, then GLB and Grasshopper

"A configurable engineering layer upstream of Revit."

That was the shift we designed for: from idea generation to a tool inside production workflows. Real estate developers became the fastest-converting segment.

What's next

The moment a user runs out of free credits. 925 people viewed pricing, 69 clicked "get plan," and 5 to 10 users a day exhaust their credits while 0 to 2 upgrade. That moment, plus Team plan and enterprise onboarding, is where design goes next.
